1. Project Overview & Problem Statement

Commercial aviation maintenance operates under extreme financial and safety constraints. Unscheduled engine groundings—known as Aircraft on Ground (AOG) events—cost airlines between $150,000 and $300,000 per hour in flight delays, passenger compensation, and emergency logistics. Traditional aviation maintenance relies primarily on static, interval-based schedules (such as A, B, and C-checks every 500–6,000 flight hours) and conservative redline threshold alarms.

However, interval-based checks fail to detect early internal thermo-mechanical degradation, while single-sensor redline alarms trigger only after physical damage (such as rotor blade tip rub, seal leakage, or compressor aerodynamic stall) has already occurred.

The Digital Twin Solution: This project implements an industrial-grade Digital Twin of the CFM56-5B4 commercial turbofan engine. By creating a synchronized computational mirror of physical engine telemetry, the system continuously tracks 21 sensor channels across 6 thermodynamic stations. It pairs dual-track predictive machine learning (LSTM sequence regression for Remaining Useful Life and unsupervised Isolation Forest for anomaly detection) with Explainable AI (TreeSHAP) and statistical threshold analysis. This converts "black-box" neural network outputs into human-understandable physical diagnoses for flight-line maintenance crews.

2. NASA C-MAPSS Dataset Architecture

The project is built on the gold-standard NASA Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation (C-MAPSS) dataset developed by the NASA Glenn Research Center. The simulator accurately models a 90,000-lb thrust commercial dual-spool turbofan engine with transient flight envelope dynamics.

Why Keeping All 21 Sensors Matters (Professor / Viva Defense): In FD001, 7 sensors (T2, P2, farB, epr, Nf_dmd, PCNfR_dmd, P15) have near-zero variance due to constant sea-level operation. Naive pipelines drop them. However, in FD002 and FD004, these sensors vary significantly with altitude and Mach number. We retain all 21 sensors across all datasets so our models remain generalizable across flight envelopes. TreeSHAP mathematically weights inactive sensors to ~0% in FD001 without manual feature pruning.

4. Deep-Dive Work Division for 4 Team Members

To ensure an equitable and technically rigorous workload distribution suitable for university assessment, the project is divided into four distinct specialization leads:

Person 1: Data Engineering & Preprocessing Lead Pipeline & Ingestion Architecture

Primary Implementation Script: src/data_loader_v2.py

  • Data Lake Ingestion: Built the whitespace-separated regex parser for all 26 C-MAPSS columns across 1,416 engines, stripping trailing null tokens.
  • Temporal Sequence Buffering: Formulated the sliding-window sequence generator with shape (Batch, 50 cycles, 21 sensors), strictly isolating engine boundaries to eliminate temporal data leakage between distinct engine IDs.
  • Piecewise Linear RUL Target Generation: Created ground-truth RUL curves capped at 125 cycles (representing the initial stable operation phase where degradation has not yet begun).
  • Fleet Normalization: Implemented MinMaxScaler fitted solely on healthy initial cycles (cycles 1 to 20) and exported parameter scalers for real-time inverse transforms.
Viva Defense Question: "Why use a 50-cycle sliding window instead of single-cycle snapshot rows?"
Answer: "Turbofan degradation is a dynamic non-linear rate of change. Single sensor snapshots contain sensor noise and flight turbulence. A 50-cycle trajectory provides the LSTM recurrent gates with temporal derivatives (dT/dt, dP/dt) necessary to capture thermodynamic divergence."
Person 2: Deep Learning & Prognostics Lead Recurrent Neural Network Architecture

Primary Implementation Script: src/rul_predictor_lstm.py

  • LSTM Architecture Design: Built a 2-layer Long Short-Term Memory network (Layer 1: 100 units, return_sequences=True; Layer 2: 50 units) with 20% Dropout regularization and Dense linear regression head.
  • NASA Asymmetric Loss Scoring: Implemented the official NASA evaluation metric, penalizing late predictions (predicting engine is healthy when it is near failure) exponentially more severely than early predictions.
  • Model Training & Validation: Trained with Adam optimizer (learning rate 0.001), EarlyStopping on validation loss, achieving RMSE of 14.8 cycles across the 100 test engines.
Viva Defense Question: "Why is the NASA asymmetric scoring metric preferred over standard RMSE?"
Answer: "In aviation, predicting an engine has 30 cycles remaining when it only has 5 leads to catastrophic in-flight failure. Predicting 5 cycles when it has 30 only causes an early scheduled inspection. Asymmetric scoring mathematically enforces flight safety."
Person 3: Anomaly Detection & Health Classification Lead Statistical Machine Learning & Health Staging

Primary Implementation Scripts: src/anomaly_detector_iforest.py, src/classification.py

  • Unsupervised Isolation Forest: Trained Isolation Forest on nominal baseline cycles (cycles 1-10) across all engines with contamination factor 0.10. Computes real-time anomaly scores via recursive binary space partitioning.
  • Multi-Class Health Stage Classifier: Trained a 100-tree Random Forest classifier categorizing engine states into:
    • Stage 0 (Healthy): RUL > 100 cycles. All sensors within 2.5-sigma envelope.
    • Stage 1 (Degrading): 30 < RUL <= 100 cycles. Sub-threshold thermodynamic drift.
    • Stage 2 (Critical): RUL <= 30 cycles. Redline thresholds breached, immediate action.
  • Fleet Threshold Calibration: Statistically derived 2.5-sigma warning and 3.8-sigma redline limits across healthy fleet distributions, achieving 0% false alarms on Cycle 1.
Viva Defense Question: "Why combine unsupervised Isolation Forest with supervised Random Forest?"
Answer: "Isolation Forest detects novel, zero-day anomalous patterns without needing ground-truth failure labels, while Random Forest provides deterministic operational health stages for maintenance planning."
Person 4: Explainable AI & Digital Twin Dashboard Lead Explainability, API & Full-Stack Interface

Primary Implementation Scripts: src/feature_importance_shap.py, src/dashboard_api.py, dashboard/src/

  • Explainable AI (TreeSHAP): Implemented Shapley Additive Explanations computing exact marginal feature contributions. Identified Ps30 (14.2%), Nc (13.1%), P30 (9.8%), NRc (9.4%), and phi (9.3%) as primary HPC failure drivers.
  • CFM56-5B4 Vector Cutaway Schematic: Recreated the exact physical engine cross-section in responsive SVG from technical drawing designrefrence/techdraw-file.pdf, mapping 6 stations to real mechanical coordinates.
  • Human-in-the-Loop Diagnostic Translation: Built the threshold analysis meter, plain-English mechanical root cause explanations, and prescriptive maintenance work orders.
  • Full-Stack Architecture: Developed Flask REST API backend (localhost:5000) and React + Vite frontend (localhost:5173).
Viva Defense Question: "Why is Explainable AI necessary if the LSTM already predicts RUL accurately?"
Answer: "A maintenance technician cannot disassemble a $15 million jet engine based on an opaque RUL number. SHAP explains which specific component (e.g. HPC rotor vs Combustor) caused the deterioration, pinpointing exactly where to insert the borescope."

5. The Prediction Pipeline: What Goes In, What Comes Out

Pipeline Stage Data Input Format & Shape Transformation / Processing Output Format & Shape
1. Telemetry Ingestion Raw C-MAPSS logs: 26 whitespace columns (EngineID, Cycle, Op_1..3, s_1..21) Regex tokenization, trailing null stripping, engine continuity check Cleaned Pandas DataFrame: Shape (N, 26)
2. Scaling & Windowing Sensor matrix: Shape (N, 21) in standard engineering units MinMaxScaler normalization, 50-step sliding window buffer per engine Tensor: Shape (Batch, 50, 21)
3. LSTM RUL Prediction Tensor: (1, 50, 21) 2-layer LSTM recurrent forward pass + dropout + linear activation Scalar: RUL in remaining cycles (e.g. 191 cycles)
4. Anomaly Detection Single-cycle vector: (1, 21) Isolation Forest ensemble path-length evaluation against baseline Boolean (is_anomaly) + Score (s in [-1, 1])
5. Health Classification Single-cycle vector: (1, 21) Random Forest 100-tree Gini impurity voting Discrete Stage: 0 (Healthy), 1 (Degrading), 2 (Critical)
6. SHAP Attribution Single-cycle vector: (1, 21) TreeSHAP marginal contribution computation across feature trees Dictionary of 21 sensor weights summing to 100%
7. Physical Translation Normalized vector + Scaler parameters + Sensor Metadata Inverse scaling to physical units, 2.5-sigma warning / 3.8-sigma redline evaluation Station health badges, needle meter position, plain-English diagnosis

6.2 CFM56-5B4 Interactive Engine Cross-Section

Modeled directly from the CFM56-5B4 engineering technical drawing (designrefrence/techdraw-file.pdf), representing an overall engine length of 3620.4 mm. The diagram maps 6 thermodynamic stations:

  1. Station 1 (FAN / Inlet): Fan spinner, composite titanium fan blades, inlet cowl acoustic liner. Houses sensors T2, P2, Nf, NRf, Nf_dmd, PCNfR_dmd.
  2. Station 2 (LPC - Low-Pressure Compressor): 3-stage axial booster compressor and bypass duct split. Houses sensors T24, BPR.
  3. Station 3 (HPC - High-Pressure Compressor): 9-stage axial compressor with dual concentric high-pressure shaft. Primary wear zone in FD001. Houses sensors T30, P30, Ps30, Nc, NRc.
  4. Station 4 (COMB - Combustion Chamber): Annular combustor liner with 20 dual-fuel nozzles and swirlers. Houses sensors phi, farB, htBleed.
  5. Station 5 (HPT - High-Pressure Turbine): Single-stage cooled turbine driving the N2 high-pressure spool. Houses sensor W31.
  6. Station 6 (LPT - Low-Pressure Turbine): 4-stage uncooled turbine driving the N1 fan spool and exhaust cone. Houses sensors T50, W32, P15, epr.
Figure 6.3: Digital Twin Interface at Flight Cycle 192 — Critical Threshold Breach (RUL: 0 cycles, redline alarm banner, HPC thermal runaway).

6.3 Dynamic Threshold Breach Alert Banner

In nominal health (Figure 6.1), the banner remains completely hidden. When sensor drift exceeds safety thresholds (Figure 6.3), the banner activates across the top of the interface:

  • Warning Banner (Amber): Signals early degradation (2.5-sigma drift). Informs crew of scheduled inspection requirements.
  • Critical Redline Banner (Red): Signals immediate flight-safety hazard (3.8-sigma breach). Recommends grounding the aircraft and displays a direct shortcut button (Inspect HPC) to focus on the affected engine station.

6.5 Physical Root Cause & Sensor Threshold Analysis Panel (Figure 6.4)

The core innovation requested by maintenance teams to eliminate black-box uncertainty:

  • Primary Driver Identification: Automatically isolates the specific sensor experiencing the most severe thermodynamic anomaly (e.g. T30 or Ps30).
  • Tri-Color Threshold Indicator Bar: Visualizes nominal zone (0-70%, green), caution zone (70-100%, amber), and critical redline (>100%, red) with an active pointer displaying real reading vs. redline limit.
  • Plain-English Diagnostic Explanation: Explains the thermodynamic mechanism (e.g. "HPC exit temperature elevation indicates aerodynamic boundary layer separation, blade tip rubbing, and stage compressor degradation.").
  • Target Component for Borescope Inspection: Directly names the aircraft hardware part to inspect (e.g. "HPC Stages 7-9 Rotor Blades & Casing Honeycomb Shroud") with a Locate on Engine button.

6.6 Prescriptive Maintenance Work Orders

  • Nominal (RUL > 100): Recommends continuing standard flight operations; next scheduled A-check in 50 cycles.
  • Caution (30 < RUL <= 100): Recommends pre-ordering replacement compressor rotor kits and scheduling borescope inspection at the next overnight layover.
  • Critical (RUL <= 30): Generates an immediate grounding recommendation, directing aircraft maintenance technicians to remove the engine for shop-level overhaul.

6.7 RUL Trajectory Trend & Timeline Slider

  • SVG Trajectory Sparkline: Graphs the rolling 50-cycle degradation trajectory and calculates instantaneous decay rate (e.g. -1.15 cycles/flight).
  • Timeline Scrubbing Bar: Located in the footer, allows flight planners to scrub across past sorties to replay the historical progression of engine wear.

7. Summary & Project Deliverables

The C-MAPSS CFM56-5B4 Digital Twin bridges advanced data warehousing, deep learning, and practical aeronautical engineering. By combining 21-sensor sequence modeling, statistical anomaly detection, TreeSHAP explainability, and interactive physical cross-section visualization, the project delivers an end-to-end predictive maintenance platform capable of preventing unplanned in-flight shutdowns while eliminating false alarms.
